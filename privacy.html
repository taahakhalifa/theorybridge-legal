Privacy Policy — Theory Bridge

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Theory Bridge ("the app") is a UK driving theory comprehension app designed for adult learners. It is run by Khalifa Digital Ltd ("we", "us"). This privacy policy explains what data we collect, how we use it, and your rights.

We believe in keeping things simple and transparent — just like our approach to learning.

Who we are

Theory Bridge is run by Khalifa Digital Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales (company number 17513819). Its registered office, the company's official address, is 68 North Western Street, Manchester, M12 6DX.

Khalifa Digital Ltd is the data controller for the personal data this policy describes: we decide how and why it is used, and we are responsible for looking after it under UK data protection law.

What data do we collect?

We collect only the data needed to provide your personalised learning experience:

Data Why we need it
Email address To create and secure your account
Learning preferences Your language, confidence level, and topic preferences (set during onboarding) to tailor your experience
Study progress Topics studied, questions answered, scores, and accuracy — so we can track your improvement
Vocabulary mastery Which keywords you have learned, are learning, or still need to review
Mistake records Questions you got wrong, so we can help you review and retry them using spaced repetition
Mock test results Your mock test scores and topic breakdowns, so you can see how close you are to passing
Daily streak The date you last used the app, to calculate your study streak
Purchases If you buy Theory Bridge Pro: what you bought, when, and until when it lasts. RevenueCat keeps it for us, so Pro unlocks on every phone you sign in on (see Purchases below)
Crash reports Details of an error or crash in the app, so we can find and fix it (see Crash reports below)
Usage analytics (only if you agree) How you use the app, so we can see what helps learners and improve it (see Usage analytics below)

We do not collect your name, phone number, location, contacts, photos, or any other personal data beyond what is listed above.

How do we use your data?

Your data is used solely to:

  • Provide your personalised learning experience
  • Track your study progress and identify weak areas
  • Power features like spaced repetition, vocabulary mastery, and mock test history
  • Show you meaningful statistics (accuracy, streak, improvement over time)
  • If you agree to usage analytics, understand how the app is used so we can improve it
  • If you buy Theory Bridge Pro, unlock it on your account, and count how many people buy it

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling, or any purpose unrelated to your learning or what you've bought.

Usage analytics (only if you agree)

When you set up the app, we ask: "Help us improve Theory Bridge?" If you say yes, the app sends information about how you use it to PostHog, an analytics service, which keeps it on its EU servers in Frankfurt, Germany. If you say no, or don't answer, the app sends PostHog nothing at all.

Your answer is saved on your phone for your account only. If someone else signs in on the same phone, nothing about them is sent unless they say yes themselves. Nothing is sent while nobody is signed in. On another phone, analytics stay off until you turn them on there.

If you say yes, the app sends:

  • What you do in the app: the screens you open, the setup steps you complete, the answers you submit (the topic, whether you were right, which help you used and how long you took), the help buttons you tap, your mock test scores, the lessons you finish, mistakes you clear, words you learn and badges you earn, and each time you open and close the Theory Bridge Pro offer, which version of it you saw and what you bought, if anything. When you finish setup, it also sends your starting support mode and how many times you've taken the theory test before.
  • Your learning profile: your confidence level, support mode, learning preference and level.
  • Your account ID: a random code that links this information to your account. It isn't your email address.
  • Basic device details: such as your phone model, its system version, its language and time zone, and the app's version.

The app never sends PostHog your name, your email address or your password.

Like any online service, PostHog sees your device's IP address when the app sends it data. It is set not to store it, and the app asks it not to use it to work out where you are when it records what you do, so PostHog doesn't record your country or city.

We use this only to understand which parts of the app help learners and which need work. It is never used for advertising, and never sold.

Changing your mind: you can turn usage analytics on or off at any time in Settings → Usage Analytics. When you turn it off, the app stops collecting it straight away, and deletes from your phone anything it hadn't sent yet.

Deleting your analytics: deleting your account deletes the analytics linked to it too. Before we delete your account, we ask PostHog to delete your analytics profile, which is linked to your account ID, and every event recorded under it. PostHog then finishes removing them in the background. If we can't confirm the deletion with PostHog, your account isn't deleted, so you can try again. Analytics the app sent just before it stopped can reach PostHog a little later, so for about an hour afterwards, and a while longer if we keep finding some, we check again and delete anything that has arrived. Those checks stop after a few hours, so if PostHog can't be reached all that time, analytics that arrived late may remain: email us and we'll have them deleted. To delete your analytics without deleting your account, turn usage analytics off and email us at support@theorybridge.co.uk: we'll ask PostHog to delete them within 30 days of your request.

Purchases

You can buy Theory Bridge Pro in the app. Apple (on an iPhone) or Google (on an Android phone) takes the payment, under your Apple or Google account. We never see your card or bank details.

To unlock Pro, the app uses RevenueCat, a US company that handles in-app purchases, on servers in the United States. That's outside the UK. RevenueCat's data processing agreement with us includes the UK's standard data transfer terms (the International Data Transfer Addendum), which require it to protect your data to UK standards. RevenueCat receives:

  • Your account ID: the same random code as above, so a purchase follows your account to another phone. It isn't your email address.
  • Your purchases: what you bought, when, its price and currency, and whether it's still active, which Apple or Google tell it.
  • Basic device details: such as your phone's system version, its language and the app's version, and on an iPhone the ID Apple gives the app for your phone. Like any online service, RevenueCat sees your device's IP address when the app contacts it.

The app doesn't contact RevenueCat until you first sign in on the phone. After you sign out, RevenueCat knows the phone only by a new random code that isn't linked to your account. The app never sends RevenueCat your name, email address, password or anything about your learning. Our own database keeps only whether your account has Pro, and until when.

We use this only to give you what you paid for, and to see how many people buy Pro. It is never used for advertising, and never sold.

Deleting your purchase record: deleting your account deletes the record RevenueCat keeps for it too. Straight after we delete your account, we ask RevenueCat to delete everything it holds under your account ID. If RevenueCat doesn't confirm it, we keep asking automatically until it does. If your account can't be deleted, RevenueCat keeps your purchase record, so you don't lose what you bought. Deleting your account doesn't cancel a subscription, so cancel it in your Apple or Google account settings first. If a subscription was still running when you deleted your account, or you were also signed in on another phone, RevenueCat may receive new details about your account afterwards. Email us at support@theorybridge.co.uk and we'll delete them within 30 days of your request. Apple or Google keep their own record of the payment, under their own privacy policies.

Crash reports

When something goes wrong in the app, such as a crash, or progress that didn't save, the app sends a crash report to Sentry, an error-reporting service, which keeps it on its EU servers in Germany. We use it only to find and fix problems.

A crash report contains:

  • What went wrong: the error, where in the app it happened, and the screens and steps that led up to it.
  • Basic device details: your phone model, its system version, and the app's version.
  • A random code for the app on your phone, so we can tell whether one problem affects one phone or many. It isn't linked to your account.

A crash report never contains your account ID, name, email address, password or answers. Sentry sees your device's IP address when a report arrives, as any online service does, but is set not to store it. Sentry keeps crash reports for no more than 90 days.

Data storage and security

Your data is stored securely using Supabase, which keeps our database, sign-in and files on Amazon Web Services (AWS) servers in London, in the UK. All data is transmitted over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). Our database uses Row Level Security, meaning each user can only access their own data.

We take appropriate technical measures to protect your data from unauthorised access, loss, or misuse.

To protect against loss, we may keep backup copies of the database. They're encrypted before they're stored, on GitHub, and we keep each copy for no more than 21 days. We make each copy on a temporary GitHub server, which GitHub deletes when the job ends. The copy is on that server for a short time before it's encrypted. GitHub doesn't let us choose where its servers are: they may be in the United States or other countries, so outside the UK. GitHub says it protects personal data sent from the UK under the UK Extension to the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (the "UK-US data bridge"), which UK law recognises.

Who handles your data

We don't sell your data, or share it with anyone for advertising. These services handle it on our behalf:

  • Supabase hosts our database, sign-in and file storage, on servers in London, in the UK (see Data storage and security). It holds your account and everything in What data do we collect?, apart from usage analytics, crash reports and RevenueCat's purchase record.
  • PostHog handles usage analytics, only if you agree (see Usage analytics).
  • Sentry receives crash reports (see Crash reports).
  • RevenueCat, a US company, handles purchases of Theory Bridge Pro, on servers in the United States (see Purchases).
  • GitHub, a US company, runs the job that makes our database backups, and stores them, encrypted, for no more than 21 days each, on servers that may be outside the UK (see Data storage and security).
  • Expo, a US company, delivers updates to the app. Each time the app starts, it asks Expo's servers, which may be outside the UK, whether a newer version is ready. That request carries your device's IP address, the app's version and platform, and a random ID the app keeps on your phone for updates. The ID isn't linked to your account, and nothing about you or your learning is sent.
  • Pexels: hazard perception practice currently uses a sample video, which the app streams from Pexels. Like any website, Pexels sees your device's IP address when the video loads. Nothing else is sent to it.

Two more services help us prepare the app's content in advance: Google Cloud text-to-speech turns questions into the audio recordings you can play, and Anthropic (the Claude AI model) translates questions into Urdu, Polish and Kurdish. They only ever receive the app's learning content, such as questions and explanations, so they don't process any personal data about you.

We don't use advertising networks, data brokers or social media integrations.

Data retention

We keep your data for as long as your account is active. If you stop using the app, your data remains available should you return.

If you delete your account, we will delete all your personal data within 30 days. That includes any encrypted backup copies, which we keep for no more than 21 days each. Your usage analytics and your purchase record are handled separately. Before your account is deleted, we ask PostHog to delete your analytics, and PostHog finishes removing them in the background (see Usage analytics). Once it's deleted, we ask RevenueCat to delete your purchase record (see Purchases). Crash reports aren't linked to your account, so they're deleted on their own, within 90 days (see Crash reports).

Your rights

Under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, you have the right to:

  • Access your data — request a copy of the data we hold about you
  • Correct your data — ask us to fix any inaccuracies
  • Delete your data — request full deletion of your account and all associated data
  • Restrict processing — ask us to limit how we use your data
  • Data portability — request your data in a portable format
  • Object — object to any processing of your data
  • Withdraw consent — turn usage analytics off at any time in Settings

To exercise any of these rights, contact us by email or by post (see Contact us below).

If you're unhappy with how we use your data, you have the right to complain to us, by email or by post (see Contact us below). You also have the right to complain to the Information Commission (ICO). The ICO is the independent body that protects data rights in the UK. Website: https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint. Phone: 0303 123 1113. You can complain to the ICO at any time. If you contact us first, we'll try to put things right.

Account and data deletion

You can delete your account and all associated data directly from the Settings screen within the app. Upon successful completion of the deletion request, all your progress, answers, vocabulary, and mock test data will be removed. If the deletion request fails, you will be notified and your data will remain until the issue is resolved. Deleting your account in the app deletes your usage analytics and RevenueCat's purchase record too (see Usage analytics and Purchases). It doesn't cancel a subscription: cancel it in your Apple or Google account settings.

Alternatively, you can request deletion by emailing:

support@theorybridge.co.uk

We will process email deletion requests within 30 days.

Children

Cookies and device storage

The app does not use cookies. We use minimal secure device storage on your device for app functionality (such as authentication tokens and each account's usage analytics choice). To get app updates, the app also keeps a random ID that isn't linked to your account (see Who handles your data). Once you're signed in, RevenueCat keeps a copy of your account's purchases on your phone, so Pro stays unlocked when you're offline (see Purchases). The app doesn't start PostHog, and PostHog stores nothing on your phone, unless you agree to usage analytics. If you do, it stores a random ID and its own settings, and holds events there briefly until they're sent. If you turn analytics off later, or sign out, it deletes the events it hadn't sent and sends nothing more, and the next time the app starts without your agreement, it deletes everything PostHog stored. No tracking cookies or third-party cookies are used.
